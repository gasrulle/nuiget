### Design-advisor opening ruling of 2026-11-18

Brief: the row's change and tests, `main.css:132-148`, `MainShell.tsx:109-110` and `:267`, `useSplitBounds.ts`, `layers.ts:55-112`,
`layout.ts:110-128`, interaction model §5, the workbench readings; options 1 (the shell's observer), 2 (a `focusout` on the pane),
3 (both); the exemplar question. Answer verbatim:

> Brief check first: the tree is not untouched. `git status` shows ` M docs/plans/STATUS-ux.md` (P4.fix-6 set `in-progress`, P4.fix-5's SHAs extended), and the row's note `docs/plans/handoff/P4.fix-6.md` does not exist yet. No source edit; HEAD is 699b2edf as stated. Neither blocks the ruling.
>
> DECISION: Option 1, narrowed. The shell owns the move through its own ResizeObserver on the split. It acts only on a shown-to-hidden transition of the details pane, only when the focus was in the pane, and only while the document has the focus; otherwise it pays on the document's next focus gain. Option 2's `focusout` is used only to clear a record, never as the trigger. The exemplar is not measured; the target rests on the tracker row and §5, not on the workbench.
>
> WHY:
> - **Exemplar:** no committed reading of "a focused part is hidden" exists. `docs/design/workbench/*.json` holds list-focus states only. The nearest reading, `[focus-open] toggle` of run 44149001126 (DECISIONS rows of 2026-11-17), has the editor focused, not the hidden part. The research documents do not cite `layout.ts`. The exemplar stays "from knowledge".
> - **Target:** it stands on P4.4's opening ruling DO 18, the tracker row, and §5's Ctrl+DownArrow sentence ("the active row of the visible list (the tab's list, or the Browse results)"). `focusList` already implements that sentence.
> - **Why not `restoreFocus` alone:** its `HIDDEN` literal does not cover `display: none`, and an empty visible list would fall straight to the first control of the page. So `focusList` goes first and `restoreFocus` is the fallback. The details feature renders no listbox or tree (grep of `src/webview/main/features/details` is empty), so the fallback walk cannot break on a hidden list.
> - **Why the focus guard:** a `focus()` in a webview pulls the workbench focus into its frame (gotcha "A webview focusing an element at mount", UX-P3.8). The precedent is `SidebarView.tsx:83-107` (SB14).
> - **Why transition-only:** a panel that opens narrow with `<body>` active must claim nothing. Otherwise every narrow capture cell would gain a ringed row and DS-FOCUS-2 goldens would move.
> - **Why a record:** whether Chromium runs the focus fixup before or after the ResizeObserver callback, and whether it fires `focusout`, is unverified. The handler accepts both orders.
>
> DO:
> 1. Write `docs/plans/handoff/P4.fix-6.md` and paste this ruling there and into the directory `STATUS.md`.
> 2. Add one hook beside `useSplitBounds.ts` (for example `src/webview/main/useHiddenDetailsFocus.ts`), called from `MainShell` with `splitRef` and a `moveFocus` callback. It keeps three refs: `shown` (pane rendered at the last reading), `inPane`, and `owed`.
> 3. Read "pane hidden" from the CSS, not a width: `getComputedStyle(pane).display === 'none'` on `.nuiget-main-details` inside the split.
> 4. Keep `inPane` with a `focusin` listener on the document: `inPane = pane.contains(target)`.
> 5. Clear `inPane` on a `focusout` from the pane only when `relatedTarget` is null, the pane is still rendered and `document.hasFocus()` (the user left to nowhere). A `focusout` while the pane is hidden or the document is unfocused changes nothing.
> 6. In the hook's own `ResizeObserver` on the split, do not call at mount: the first callback only sets `shown`.
> 7. On a callback where `shown` was true and the pane is now hidden, set `owed` when the active element is inside the pane, or when it is null, `<body>` or `<html>` and `inPane` is true. Then update `shown`.
> 8. Pay through one `settle()`: when `owed`, the pane is still hidden, `document.hasFocus()`, and the active element is in the pane or nowhere, call `moveFocus()` and clear `owed`.
> 9. Call `settle()` from the observer callback and from a `window` `focus` listener.
